AI fills in what you leave blank

Every blank becomes a decision. Keep the ones that matter and hand the rest over on purpose. Specificity beats volume: a useful detail rules out wrong answers, and extra words don't.

AI is not a GPS

A GPS reroutes you but keeps your destination. AI can change both, one reasonable-looking step at a time, and explain why the new destination was better. One great question or twenty ordinary ones both get there, as long as the destination doesn't move.

Context: what does the AI need to know?

  • Goal and success criteria
  • Audience and purpose
  • Source documents and evidence
  • Examples of good or bad output
  • Constraints and non-negotiables
  • History, previous decisions, what's been tried
  • Policies or rules
  • Tone, format, and where it will be used

Steer casually

  • That is not what I meant.
  • Keep this part and change the rest.
  • What am I missing?
  • Ask me the questions you need before continuing.
  • We are drifting from the original goal.
  • Give me three genuinely different approaches.
  • What assumptions are you making?
  • Critique this against the goal we established.
  • Go back to what we decided earlier.

Let it interview you

I need help with this: [your task]. When this is finished, [who] will be able to [what]. Before you write anything, ask me the five questions whose answers would most change the result. Ask one at a time.

Conversation is not failure. The middle turns are often where you find out what you really need.

When things seem off, start a new conversation

  • It brings back a mistake you already fixed
  • It forgets something you decided
  • It changes things you didn't ask about
  • Answers get longer, vaguer, more generic
  • You're arguing instead of building

Carry the decisions forward, not the clutter:

Summarize what we've decided so far: the goal, the decisions we've locked in, the context that matters, and what's still open. Write it so I can paste it into a brand-new conversation and pick up exactly where we left off.

Some problems are bigger than one prompt

Understand, Gather, Evaluate, Plan, Create, Review, Revise, Deliver

Different stages need different context and different tools:

  • Files to gather your sources
  • Data analysis to evaluate numbers
  • Web search for current facts, with links you check
  • Voice to get context out of your head
  • Images for visual ideas

Start at the end. Design backward from finished. For each stage: what does AI do, and what do I decide?

AI doesn't have to do every step

Good jobs for AI: drafts, summaries, options, reformatting, first-pass analysis, critique against your goal.

Stays with you: the destination, priorities, approvals, ethical calls, the facts you stand behind, and responsibility for the result. A prompt is a component, not the system.

Now write the perfect prompt

Review everything we did in this conversation. Write the single prompt that could have produced something close to our final result from the beginning. Then explain which parts of that prompt mattered most and why.

Then test it in a new chat. If it gets close, save it. If not, you've learned how much the conversation was doing.

Before anything leaves your screen

  • Is it still headed to your destination?
  • Check every name, number, date, and source.
  • Remove anything it made up or can't support.
  • Don't paste confidential, personal, or customer information into tools you haven't been cleared to use.
  • You read it. You sign off.

My notes

High-impact prompting isn't the art of finding magic words. It's knowing the outcome you want, keeping the decisions that matter, giving AI the context it needs, steering it through a conversation, and building a workflow that reliably gets you there. AI can help find the route. You own the destination.
