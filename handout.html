Set the destination

I'll know it's right when  .

One excellent prompt or twenty ordinary ones can both get there, as long as the destination doesn't move.

AI is not a GPS

A GPS reroutes you but keeps your destination. AI can change the destination, one reasonable-looking step at a time, and then explain why the new one was better. Every few turns, check where it's taking you.

Ask for a change, then look for what else changed that you didn't ask for.

Context the AI might need

  • Goal and what success looks like
  • Audience and purpose
  • Source documents and real facts
  • Examples of good, or bad
  • Constraints and non-negotiables
  • History and earlier decisions
  • What's already been tried
  • Rules and policies
  • Tone, format, and where it will be used

Long isn't the same as good. Skip anything that wouldn't change the result.

Steering phrases

  • That's not what I meant.
  • Keep this part and change the rest.
  • What am I missing?
  • Ask me the questions you need before continuing.
  • We're drifting from the original goal.
  • Go back to what we decided earlier.
  • Give me three genuinely different approaches.
  • What assumptions are you making?
  • Critique this against the goal we established.

When things seem off, start fresh

  • It brings back a mistake you already fixed
  • It forgets something you decided
  • It changes things you didn't ask about
  • Answers get longer, vaguer, more generic
  • You're arguing instead of building

Long conversations get crowded and eventually run out of room. Carry the decisions forward, not the clutter:

Summarize what we've decided so far: the goal, the decisions we've locked in, the context that matters, and what's still open. Write it so I can paste it into a brand-new conversation and pick up exactly where we left off.

Paste it into a new chat and re-attach your files.

Start at the end

For bigger work, begin with "what does finished look like?" and work backward. What has to exist right before that? And before that?

Understand, Gather, Evaluate, Plan, Create, Review, Revise, Deliver

For each stage, ask:

  • What's the goal of this stage?
  • What context does it need?
  • What should AI do?
  • What should I decide?
  • How will I know it's done?

A prompt is a component, not the system. You keep the approvals and the responsibility.

Turn a good conversation into a reusable prompt

Review everything we did in this conversation. Write the single prompt that could have produced something close to our final result from the beginning. Then explain which parts of that prompt mattered most and why.

Then test it in a new chat. If it gets close, save it. If it doesn't, you've learned how much the conversation was doing.

Before anything leaves your screen

  • Is it still headed to your destination?
  • Check every name, number, date, and source.
  • Remove anything it made up or can't support.
  • Don't paste confidential, personal, or customer information into a tool your organization hasn't approved.
  • You read it. You sign off.

High-impact prompting isn't about finding magic words. It's knowing the outcome you want, keeping the decisions that matter, giving the AI the context it needs, and steering until you get there. AI can help find the route. You own the destination.
