Before we start.

Open ChatGPT in a browser tab and keep this page open next to it. You'll go back and forth between them all morning.

Everything you type on this page stays in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere. Your answers build up into My task, a one-page plan you can print or save as a PDF at the end.

One rule for today. Don't paste anything confidential into ChatGPT: personal information, student or customer records, passwords, or anything your employer wouldn't want outside the building. If your real task involves that kind of material, describe it instead of pasting it, or use the practice company in section 6.

No ChatGPT account, or you've hit the image limit? Pair up with a neighbor for the image exercises. Everything after section 4 works on any AI chat tool, including Copilot and Claude.

1

A bowl of fruit.

10 minutes. Do this before we talk about anything.

Try it

  1. Start a new chat in ChatGPT.
  2. Type exactly this. Don't add a word.
Create an image of a bowl of fruit.
  1. Compare yours with the people around you. Then look around the room.
  2. For each choice below, mark who actually made it.

You made 0 of 8 decisions.

Talk about it

  • Which of those decisions actually mattered to you?
  • The images aren't identical. Are they really different, or just versions of the same safe default?
  • What happens when millions of people let the same system make the same choices?

AI fills in what you leave blank. Leave everything blank and you get a competent result that looks like everyone else's.

2

Give it a purpose.

8 minutes

The fix is not "add more words." It's deciding what the image is for, then keeping only the two or three choices that purpose actually depends on.

Try it

Pick a purpose. Then fill in no more than three decisions that matter for it, and leave the rest to the AI on purpose.

Run it in the same chat as your first bowl. You'll keep editing this image in the next section.

Talk about it

  • Compare with someone who picked a different purpose. How much did three choices change?
  • Which choice did the most work? Which one could you have left out?

Specificity beats volume. A useful detail rules out wrong answers. Extra words don't.

3

Five small edits.

12 minutes

A GPS reroutes you when you miss a turn, but it never changes your destination. AI can change both, and then explain why the new destination was better all along. This exercise lets you watch it happen.

Try it

  1. Stay in the same chat as your purpose image.
  2. Ask for five edits, one at a time. Each one changes one thing only and ends with "Keep everything else exactly the same."
  3. After each edit, write down anything else that changed that you didn't ask for.

Need ideas? Tap one to copy it.

What I asked to changeWhat else changed anyway

AI is not a GPS. It can change the destination, one reasonable-looking step at a time.

Each edit is a fresh generation guided by everything said so far. Small, unrequested changes stack up. Drift is easy to see in an image. It's just as real in an email, a plan, or a spreadsheet, where it's much harder to spot.

4

Start fresh.

5 minutes

Long conversations get crowded. The model is juggling more and more, and your earliest decisions carry less weight. Eventually the conversation runs out of room entirely. You'll usually feel it before you hit that wall.

When things seem off

  • It brings back a mistake you already fixed
  • It forgets something you decided earlier
  • It changes things you didn't ask about
  • Answers get longer, vaguer, or more generic
  • You're arguing with it instead of building
  • You can't remember what you agreed on either

Try it

Don't throw the work away. Carry the decisions forward, not the clutter. In your bowl-of-fruit chat, send this:

Summarize what we've decided so far: the goal, the decisions we've locked in, the context that matters, and what's still open. Write it so I can paste it into a brand-new conversation and pick up exactly where we left off.
  1. Open a new chat.
  2. Paste the summary. Re-attach any files you were using.
  3. Ask for the image again. Is it closer to what you meant than edit five was?

When it starts to feel off, start a new conversation and bring your decisions with you.

5

Know the destination.

10 minutes. Your task starts here.

You can get somewhere useful with one excellent prompt or twenty ordinary ones. Both work if the destination stays put. The skill isn't wording. It's knowing where you're going well enough to notice when the AI isn't taking you there.

You don't need to know how to get there. You do need to know what "there" is.

Vague destination"Help me with onboarding."
Clear destination"When this is finished, our shift lead can run a new hire's first week from one page."

Pick your real task

Choose something you actually need to finish: an email, a plan, a document, a schedule, a decision, a training. You'll work on it for the rest of the morning.

6

Context.

15 minutes

Stop asking "what words should I use?" and start asking "what does the AI need to know to help me succeed?" That's context. A long prompt isn't automatically a good one. Useful context rules out wrong paths. Everything else is noise.

Same task, different context

Flat Cap Co. makes camouflage flat caps for the whole family, pets included. They're changing their return policy.

Round one. New chat. Just this:

Write an announcement about a policy change.

Round two. New chat. Choose who it's for, then copy the prompt. The memo below is already included.

Memo from Felix Bramble, Operations. November 2, 2026.

Returns are changing on December 1. Every return will get one of five reason codes: Fit, Color or pattern, Damaged, Wrong item, Changed mind. Support staff choose the code. Customers no longer type in their own reason.

Why: First-quarter returns rose 13%. "Fit not as expected" was the top reason at 38%, but reasons were logged so inconsistently that we couldn't tell what to fix.

What's new for customers: Returns for fit become a free exchange for a different size within 30 days, and we pay shipping both ways. All other returns stay refund-only with a $6 return label fee.

Staff action: Finish the 10-minute reason-code guide by November 24. Questions go to Lindsay Draper.

Talk about it

  • What did round one make up that wasn't true?
  • Compare with someone who picked the other audience. Same facts, how different is the result?
  • Which single piece of context changed the most?

Every blank becomes a decision. Context is how you decide which blanks you fill and which ones you hand over on purpose.

Now your task

Check what the AI would need for your task. Add a quick note for each one you check. Skip anything that wouldn't change the result.

Ten-minute break.

Before you go, make sure My task has a real destination. That's what you'll build after the break.

7

Steer the conversation.

15 minutes

You don't have to write the perfect prompt up front. Start with the problem, react to what comes back, add what's missing, reject what's wrong, keep what's good. Needing ten turns isn't failure. The middle turns are often where you figure out what you actually need.

Idea→Conversation→Clarify→Context→Create→Critique→Refine→Done

Watch a real cascade

A shop owner needs help onboarding new hires. Step through it and watch where the destination shows up, and where the AI tries to move it.

Steering phrases

Short and casual works. Copy these into your own conversation and check off the ones you use.

    Try it on your task

    1. New chat. Start loosely. Describe your task the way you'd tell a coworker.
    2. React to whatever comes back. Use at least three steering phrases.
    3. Every few turns, check it against your destination: set one in section 5

    The conversation is where you discover the problem. Your job is to keep the destination from moving.

    8

    Start at the end.

    12 minutes

    Once a task is bigger than one exchange, stop treating it as a prompt problem. Break it into stages, and design them backward from "finished." Different stages need different context: research needs sources, drafting needs examples and tone, review needs the goal.

    Understand→Gather→Evaluate→Plan→Create→Review→Revise→Deliver

    A prompt is a component, not the system. AI can find the route. You decide, you approve, and you own the result.

    Reverse-design your workflow

    1. Start with finished. What has to exist right before that? And right before that?
    2. For each stage, split the work: what does AI do, what do you decide, and how do you know the stage is done?

    Add a stage, or tap a suggestion.

    FinishedSet your destination in section 5

    Pick the right capability for the stage

    GatherUpload the files you already have, or turn on web search for current facts. Source material becomes the AI's working environment.
    EvaluateUpload a spreadsheet and ask for analysis. Ask it to show its work so you can check the numbers.
    CreateDrafting, rewriting, images. Give examples of what good looks like.
    ReviewAsk for a critique against the goal you set, not a general "is this good?"
    DecideStays with you. Approvals, judgment calls, anything with your name on it.

    Features change. Choosing the right capability for the stage doesn't.

    9

    Build something real.

    15 minutes

    The goal isn't to write a good prompt. It's to make real progress on something you need. This prompt is assembled from your answers so far. Open a new chat and start with it.

    Your starting prompt

    1. Paste it into a new chat. Attach source files if you have them, after checking the one rule.
    2. Work through your first stage. Steer. Keep what's good.
    3. If it starts to feel off, use the start-fresh prompt from section 4.

    Before anything leaves your screen

    • Is it still headed to your destination, or did it move?
    • Check every name, number, date, and source it gives you.
    • Remove anything it made up or can't support.
    • You read it. You sign off. Your name is on it, not the AI's.
    10

    Teach it back.

    5 minutes

    Now that you know where you were going, the "perfect prompt" is easy to write. Have the AI write it.

    Try it

    In your build conversation, send this:

    Review everything we did in this conversation. Write the single prompt that could have produced something close to our final result from the beginning. Then explain which parts of that prompt mattered most and why.

    Then test it. Paste that prompt into a new chat. How close does it get?

    If it got close, you have a reusable prompt. If it didn't, you've just seen how much the conversation was doing.
